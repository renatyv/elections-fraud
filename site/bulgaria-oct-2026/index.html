Screening does not establish ballot stuffing; some flags need review

At 100% processed, Yotova has 34.759% and Gyurov 31.952%. All 12,719 section protocols reconcile with national candidate totals. On 10,616 eligible domestic sections, Shpilkin-style tails and integer peaks do not establish stuffing, while 22 protocol-balance discrepancies and two candidate digit flags merit inspection. There is no historical control in this exploratory run. The ten night snapshots ended earlier, at 1,512,779 listed votes and 98.99% processed.

Three findings from the saved night capture

Stable ordering, a non-monotonic margin, and checks with a limited scope.

1

The leading pair never changed in the captured snapshots

At the first capture (~95,000 listed votes) Yotova had 35.5%, Gyurov 30.3%, Kostadinov 13.5%. Across the captured night, Gyurov gained ~1.7 points, Yotova lost ~0.8 and Kostadinov ~0.9; the leading pair stayed in the same order.

2

The margin narrowed, with one unexplained bounce

The Yotova − Gyurov gap went from +5.21 to +2.72 points — not monotonically: +3.13 by 02:08, back up to +4.71 at 03:07 (+1.6 points on a single update), then a steady slide to +2.56 and +2.72 at the finish. Hourly snapshots cannot distinguish a new batch of protocols from a correction of already entered ones.

3

Consistency is exact, but it is only part of an audit

At the final night snapshot, the sum of all 32 district totals equals the national listed-vote total. Rounded displayed shares sum to 99.996–100.005% across captured area-time rows, consistent with three-decimal rounding. This verifies internal arithmetic, not ballot authenticity or section-level data quality.

Two Bulgarias: provinces for Yotova, big cities and abroad for Gyurov

27 of 31 domestic districts for Yotova; her rival’s map is Sofia, Plovdiv-city and the diaspora.

Yotova leads everywhere except the three Sofia districts and Plovdiv-city. Her strongest regions are Haskovo (+29.3 points), Silistra (+28.7) and Smolyan (+26.6); Varna is a razor edge (+1.8). Gyurov polls 54.4% in Sofia-23 and leads the abroad aggregate by 22.7 points.

The abroad vote is small but was the most mobile part of the night

The abroad aggregate (MIR 32, “Обобщени данни”) holds 80,431 listed votes — 5.3% of the national total — split Gyurov 45.61 / Yotova 22.88 / Kostadinov 11.63. Its first partial snapshot had Gyurov ahead by 44.8 points, converging to +22.7 as more consular sections entered: early abroad batches are not representative of the final abroad vote.

The count-night swing sharpened an already-formed split

Between the first and last snapshots 22 of 32 areas moved toward Yotova and 10 toward Gyurov. Two provincial leads flipped sign overnight — Blagoevgrad from Gyurov +9.7 to Yotova +7.8, Varna from Gyurov +5.3 to Yotova +1.8 — while the Gyurov-ward movers are precisely his strongholds: Plovdiv-city (−17.4 points of swing), Sofia province (−10.2) and Sofia-23 (−9.4).

Very low turnout, and high-turnout districts are Gyurov’s

From 6.77% (Kardzhali) to 25.91% (Sofia-23) at 16:00; the margin correlation is mostly a big-city effect.

Official 16:00 turnout ranges from 6.77% in Kardzhali to 25.91% in Sofia-23. Across the 31 domestic districts turnout correlates with the final Yotova − Gyurov margin at r = −0.63 — but the correlation collapses to r = −0.32 once the four big-city districts are excluded: the association is mostly a big-city effect, not a smooth turnout gradient. As a rough national bound, 1,512,779 listed votes equal 22.79% of the register — a lower bound, since invalid ballots are unknown from this capture.

First validate the source: exact vote totals, 22 balance discrepancies

12,719 protocols captured; 10,616 domestic sections eligible for screening.

Individual numeric protocols are available even though the bulk open-data page and ZIP files return 404. I selected one complete protocol per section, avoiding double-counting machine components. All 27 candidate totals and the “nobody” total match the national table exactly. But 22 protocols (19 domestic, 3 abroad) have candidate-plus-nobody votes above signatures, or signatures above the opening register plus additions. For example, section 321320600 lists 248 machine votes but 56 signatures. This is a source-field discrepancy requiring protocol review; it is not proof of fabricated votes.

The screening excludes 613 abroad sections, then 19 domestic balance discrepancies, then 1,471 registers below 100 voters: 10,616 remain. Small-register filtering does not identify every special or mobile section. The exclusion list, selection audit and national reconciliation make the scope inspectable.

Shpilkin and other methods: results

Shpilkin tail, fingerprints, integer peaks, digits, local outliers and synthetic sensitivity — with explicit limits.

These tests now use actual section protocols, rather than the 32 district aggregates. The 16:00 district correlation is a separate descriptive check. Turnout here uses the opening register plus election-day additions; candidate shares use signatures/ballots cast. The subset contains 1,399,546 signatures and 490,916 Yotova votes. Her 35.08% subset share therefore differs from the national 34.759% listed-vote share.

MethodResult and interpretation
Shpilkin tailCutoff 50.48%, fitted to the same data. Tail: 7,845 Yotova votes (1.60% of her subset votes). Removing it changes her subset share from 35.08% to 35.10%. This is not an estimate of fraudulent votes; cutoffs at the 99th–99.9th percentiles give 13,303–2,445 tail votes.
Comet / Klimek fingerprintsTwo-component mixture fails the toolkit’s separation criterion. The fitted component weight 0.676 is not a fraud fraction. Weighted turnout–Yotova-share correlation: −0.140.
Kobak integer peaksInteger turnout: 1.884% observed vs 1.855% under the binomial null; z = 0.25. Yotova integer share: z = 1.55. No large integer excess under this null.
Second digit / last digitYotova’s second-digit smooth-null p = 0.693. Her last-digit p = 0.0224 (189 tallies) becomes 0.1122 after Holm correction. Vasilev and Mihaylov second-digit flags remain at adjusted p = 0.01746 each; inspect truncation and model fit before attributing them to manipulation. Correction covers seven candidate/test combinations with ≥50 usable tallies.
Local outliers / Isolation Forest388 sections exceed |z| > 4 within their municipality. Isolation Forest flags 107 with a configured 1% contamination. These are review lists; the ML rate is set by a parameter.
Synthetic injection / historical controlAdding 100,376 synthetic Yotova votes increases the tail signal 7.79×. A separate rounded-turnout injection raises z from 0.45 to 3.07. This tests sensitivity to those patterns. No previous presidential control has been run.

No previous presidential election is used as a control. Cutoffs are fitted to the current data; voting-mode and special-section stratification remain incomplete. Machine-learning flags are inspection priorities, not fraud estimates. Physical ballots, coercion, vote buying and register quality were not audited.

What this analysis cannot establish

Interim published totals are evidence about the count, not a complete election audit.

  • The ten night snapshots stop at 98.99%; the updated national table is a separate observation at 100% processing. District charts are not updated to that later endpoint. Protocol-processing percentages are not percentages of votes counted.
  • The historical charts use 32 areas; the forensic screen uses 10,616 selected domestic sections. Abroad sections and identified balance discrepancies are excluded. Register-size filtering cannot fully separate ordinary, mobile and institutional sections, and no historical control is available.
  • Hourly snapshots cannot exclude movements or corrections between captures; the 03:07 bounce is described, not explained.
  • No ballot papers, physical protocols, voter-register quality, coercion, vote buying or campaign conditions were audited. Statistical consistency of published totals cannot certify that an election was free and fair.
