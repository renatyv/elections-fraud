Bulgaria’s updated results are in.
Do the numbers add up?

!

The checks do not show added fake ballots; some findings need a closer look

With 100% of tally sheets processed, Yotova has 34.759% and Gyurov 31.952%. The candidate totals from all 12,719 polling stations add up to the national figures. I checked 10,616 domestic stations for unusual turnout and round percentages, without finding evidence of added fake ballots. But 22 tally sheets contain numbers that do not add up, and two candidates have unusual vote-count digits. These need checking. I have no earlier election for comparison. The ten saved overnight updates stop earlier, at 1,512,779 listed votes and 98.99% of sheets processed.

⇄

In the 98.99% overnight update, Yotova led in 27 of 31 domestic districts and by 2.72 percentage points nationally. Gyurov led in the three Sofia districts, Plovdiv-city and abroad. The charts below use that update. The later national result is shown separately.

Three findings from the overnight updates

The leader stayed the same, the gap briefly widened, and the totals added up.

1

Yotova stayed ahead in every saved update

In the first update, with about 95,000 listed votes, Yotova had 35.5%, Gyurov 30.3% and Kostadinov 13.5%. By the last one, Gyurov had gained about 1.7 percentage points, Yotova had lost about 0.8 and Kostadinov about 0.9. The top two stayed in the same order.

2

The gap narrowed, apart from one unexplained jump

Yotova’s lead over Gyurov fell from 5.21 to 2.72 percentage points. It reached 3.13 at 02:08, then jumped to 4.71 at 03:07 — a rise of 1.6 points in one update. It later fell to 2.56 and ended at 2.72. Hourly updates cannot tell me whether that jump came from newly entered tally sheets or corrections.

3

The totals add up, but that does not verify the ballots

In the last overnight update, all 32 district totals add up exactly to the national listed-vote total. For each saved area and time, displayed shares add up to 99.996–100.005%, as expected when figures are rounded to three decimal places. This checks the arithmetic. It does not verify the ballots or each polling station’s records.

Where each candidate led in the overnight results

Yotova led in 27 of 31 domestic districts. Gyurov led in Sofia, Plovdiv-city and abroad.

In the last overnight update, Yotova led in every domestic district except the three in Sofia and Plovdiv-city. Her largest leads were in Haskovo (29.3 percentage points), Silistra (28.7) and Smolyan (26.6). Varna was close, at 1.8 points. Gyurov had 54.4% in Sofia-23 and led the combined vote abroad by 22.7 points.

Early results abroad gave a misleadingly large lead

The combined vote abroad (MIR 32, “Обобщени данни”) has 80,431 listed votes, or 5.3% of the national total. Gyurov has 45.61%, Yotova 22.88% and Kostadinov 11.63%. Gyurov’s lead fell from 44.8 points in the first partial update to 22.7 as more overseas stations were added. The early results did not represent the eventual overseas vote.

Two districts changed leader during the saved count

Between the first and last saved updates, the gap moved toward Yotova in 22 of 32 areas and toward Gyurov in 10. Blagoevgrad changed from a 9.7-point Gyurov lead to a 7.8-point Yotova lead. Varna changed from Gyurov ahead by 5.3 to Yotova ahead by 1.8. The largest moves toward Gyurov included his strong areas: Plovdiv-city (−17.4 points in the Yotova-minus-Gyurov gap), Sofia province (−10.2) and Sofia-23 (−9.4).

Gyurov did better in districts with higher afternoon turnout

At 16:00, turnout ranged from 6.77% in Kardzhali to 25.91% in Sofia-23. Big-city districts account for much of the link.

Official turnout at 16:00 ranged from 6.77% in Kardzhali to 25.91% in Sofia-23. Across 31 domestic districts, higher turnout went with a smaller Yotova lead or a larger Gyurov lead (correlation r = −0.63). Without the four big-city districts, the link was weaker (r = −0.32). Much of the pattern therefore comes from those cities. The 1,512,779 listed votes in the last overnight update equal 22.79% of registered voters. Turnout was at least that high, since this total leaves out invalid ballots.

!
The totals add up, but 22 tally sheets contain mismatches

I saved 12,719 tally sheets and used 10,616 domestic polling stations in the statistical checks.

Individual tally-sheet numbers are available online, even though the bulk-data page and ZIP files return “not found” (404). I used one complete sheet per station, taking care not to count machine subtotals twice. The totals for all 27 candidates and “Do not support anyone” match the national table exactly. But 22 sheets (19 domestic, 3 abroad) list more candidate-plus-“nobody” votes than voters’ signatures, or more signatures than registered voters including election-day additions. For example, station 321320600 shows 248 machine votes but 56 signatures. The published fields need checking against the tally sheet; this mismatch alone does not prove invented votes.

I excluded 613 stations abroad, then 19 domestic stations with numerical mismatches, then 1,471 with fewer than 100 registered voters. That leaves 10,616. Excluding small rolls does not identify every special or mobile station. You can check the selection in the excluded-station list, selection record and comparison with national totals.

What the statistical checks found

Checks for unusual turnout, round percentages, vote-count digits and stations that differ from others. Each has limits.

These tests use polling-station tally sheets, rather than the 32 district totals. The comparison with 16:00 district turnout above is separate. For station turnout, I divide recorded voters’ signatures by the opening voter roll plus anyone added on election day. Candidate shares use the signatures, which record ballots cast, as the denominator. The selected stations have 1,399,546 signatures and 490,916 Yotova votes. Her share here is 35.08%, compared with 34.759% nationally; the national percentage uses listed votes instead.

CheckWhat I found and what it means
Votes at high turnout (Shpilkin)I chose a 50.48% turnout cutoff from these same data. Stations above it contain 7,845 Yotova votes, or 1.60% of her votes in this sample. Removing them changes her share from 35.08% to 35.10%. These are not necessarily fake votes. Moving the cutoff from the 99th to the 99.9th percentile, at the far upper end of the distribution, changes the count from 13,303 to 2,445 votes.
Groups on the turnout chart (comet / Klimek)A model fits two groups to turnout and vote share, but they are not separate enough to meet the tool’s rule. The fitted group weight, 0.676, is not a share of fraudulent votes. The weighted link between turnout and Yotova’s share is weak and negative (correlation −0.140).
Round percentages (Kobak)Whole-number turnout percentages appear in 1.884% of stations, close to the 1.855% expected under the binomial model of random variation (z = 0.25). For Yotova’s whole-number shares, z = 1.55. The z score measures distance from the model’s expectation. Neither result shows a large excess of round percentages against that model.
Second and last digits in vote countsYotova’s second digits give p = 0.693 against a smooth comparison pattern. Her last digits give p = 0.0224 across 189 counts, rising to 0.1122 after Holm correction, which allows for running several tests. The second-digit results for Vasilev and Mihaylov still stand out, each with adjusted p = 0.01746. Before linking this to manipulation, check whether excluding small counts and the chosen comparison pattern explain it. The correction covers seven candidate/test pairs with at least 50 usable counts. A p-value measures disagreement with the comparison pattern, not the probability of fraud.
Stations that differ from others (local outliers / Isolation Forest)388 stations differ sharply from others in their municipality (|z| > 4). The Isolation Forest computer model selects 107 stations because it was set to flag the most unusual 1%. These lists show where to look more closely; they do not count fraudulent stations.
Deliberately added changes and comparison with an earlier electionI added 100,376 made-up Yotova votes to a copy of the data. The high-turnout signal grew 7.79 times. In a separate test, changing turnout to round percentages raised z from 0.45 to 3.07. The checks respond to those specific simulated changes. I have not run a comparison with an earlier presidential election.

I have not compared these results with an earlier presidential election. The cutoffs come from the current data. Voting methods and special stations have not been fully separated. Computer-flagged stations need a closer look; they are not estimates of fraud. I did not inspect paper ballots or voter-roll quality, or investigate intimidation or vote buying.

What these checks cannot tell me

Published totals show how the count changed. They cannot answer every question about the election.

  • The ten overnight updates stop at 98.99% of tally sheets processed. The later national table reports 100%; the district charts use the earlier update. The percentage of tally sheets processed is not the percentage of votes counted.
  • The overnight charts cover 32 areas. The statistical tests cover 10,616 selected domestic polling stations, leaving out stations abroad and sheets with known numerical mismatches. Excluding small voter rolls does not separate every ordinary, mobile or institutional station. I have no earlier election for comparison.
  • Hourly records miss changes and corrections between updates. They show the jump at 03:07 but cannot explain it.
  • I did not inspect paper ballots, original tally sheets or voter-roll quality, or investigate intimidation, vote buying or campaign conditions. Even totals that add up cannot establish that an election was free and fair.
