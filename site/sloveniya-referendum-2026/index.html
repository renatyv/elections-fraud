Three reasons the numbers can be trusted

1

The arithmetic is perfect

All 12,332 precinct records add up exactly: YES + NO = valid votes, valid + invalid = ballots cast. And four separate counts ran at every precinct: in 93% of precincts the number of ballots is identical across all four; 99.6% differ by at most 2. Mass tampering cannot stay this consistent.

2

No anomalies where fraud would show

Precinct turnout forms one smooth bell curve that fades out by ~70%. Above 80% turnout there are only 10 tiny precincts — 0.1% of ballots. Stuffed ballot boxes leave the opposite signature: a visible mass of large precincts at 80–100% turnout. There is none. No spikes at round numbers, no digit patterns, no duplicated protocols.

3

The pattern is geography, not manipulation

The YES share rises smoothly with turnout (+4 points per 10 points) — the familiar city-versus-countryside split. Crucially, all four questions show the same gradient (correlation 0.94–0.96). If one count had been tampered with, it would break ranks with the other three. None does.

How I checked

Eight families of tests used by election statisticians worldwide, run on the official precinct data with open-source code. Summary: all clean.

✓
Anomalous-vote estimate (Shpilkin method)

Votes beyond the honest turnout decline: at most 1% of YES votes and 0.45% of NO votes — and removing them would shift any outcome by at most 0.15 points.

✓
Turnout × result scatter (“comet tail”)

One continuous cloud with a smooth gradient — no separate “tail” of high-turnout, high-YES precincts that ballot-stuffing produces.

✓
Election fingerprints (Klimek et al.)

The distribution matches honest elections; the specific 2D cluster typical of fraud (80–100% turnout) is absent.

✓
Round-number peaks (Kobak et al.)

Protocol values show no preference for whole percentages of turnout or result (all statistics ≤ 1.3) — no sign of numbers typed in by hand.

✓
Digit tests (Benford, last digit)

16 tests on vote counts: 13 fully clean, 3 borderline values with the opposite of the “fabricated numbers” profile — and not significant after multiple-testing correction.

✓
Outliers vs neighbours + machine learning

A few dozen precincts flagged — all tiny (16–342 voters) with mixed directions; a normal statistical tail, not a pattern.

✓
Duplicate protocols

Repeated result vectors occur at exactly the rate chance predicts (38–60 observed vs 37–53 expected). No copy-paste.

✓
Self-test: the toolkit actually detects fraud

I injected synthetic ballot-stuffing into the data — the estimated anomalous zone grew 12-fold and every alarm fired. The “clean” verdict is a real measurement, not a blind spot.

What this analysis cannot see

Honest limits of the method — statistics shows anomalies, not intentions.

  • Protocols and witnesses are not in the dataset. Statistics can confirm the numbers are mutually consistent; it cannot rule out manipulation that happened before the numbers existed (e.g. pressure on voters that is spread evenly).
  • 9% of ballots have no precinct breakdown (early voting, postal, abroad). They were checked by digit tests and totals only.
  • At the time of analysis 99.98% of ballots were counted (final check of abroad votes: 19 October 2026). Final numbers may move by dozens of votes — far too little to change any outcome.
  • Precincts average ~460 voters, so the round-number test is insensitive to falsifying a small share of protocols (verified by self-test). Digit tests need thousands of numbers to be powerful.
  • I am an independent researcher with no ties to the State Electoral Commission; all input files are official DVK publications and the analysis code is open source.
