What was decided on 18 May 2025

Snap election for the Assembly of the Republic (230 seats). The centre-right coalition AD won 32.7%, ahead of the Socialist Party (23.4%) and Chega (22.6%). National turnout: 64.4% — 5.97 million ballots cast.

Three reasons the numbers can be trusted

1

The high-turnout zone is physically empty

Parish turnout forms one bell curve that fades out by ~79%. Above the 78.6% boundary — the upper percentile of the 2022 and 2024 control elections — only 7,779 voters remain: 0.13% of ballots. Ballot-stuffing shifts mass precisely into the 80–100% zone; here that zone contains essentially nothing, and the same ~78–80% ceiling recurred in 2022 and 2024.

2

The leader’s result does not grow with turnout

In the turnout × result scatter, the AD cloud stays vertically compact across the whole range (weighted correlation −0.09), and the fingerprint analysis finds no “up-right” cluster. The two clusters it does find are geography — the rural north versus the south and the cities — not fraud.

3

Every flagged oddity is tiny — and the toolkit provably works

The 81 neighbour-outliers and 31 machine-learning anomalies are minute parishes with mixed directions — the most extreme is a parish with an anomalously LOW AD share. In the self-test, injecting 131,629 synthetic ballots grew the anomalous-zone estimate 18-fold and pushed the round-number statistic from z = 0.2 to 5.1: the “clean” verdict is a measurement, not a blind spot.

How I checked

Ten families of tests used by election statisticians worldwide, run on the official parish data with open-source code. Summary: all clean.

✓
Anomalous-vote estimate (Shpilkin method)

Beyond the honest decline: 2,980 AD votes = 0.15% of the party’s votes. Recounting without that zone moves the result from 32.72% to 32.71%.

✓
Turnout × result scatter (“comet tail”)

A continuous cloud with no “tail” of high-turnout, high-AD parishes — the signature ballot-stuffing produces.

✓
Election fingerprints (Klimek et al.)

The distribution matches honest elections; the 2D cluster typical of fraud (80–100% turnout with a high share) is absent. The clusters that do exist track geography.

✓
Round-number peaks (Kobak et al.)

Parishes with exactly whole-percentage turnout: 1.46% vs 1.44% expected by chance (z = 0.1); no excess of multiples of 5, none in the leader’s share either (z between −0.4 and +0.1).

✓
Digit tests (Benford, last digit)

12 tests on vote counts: 11 clean. The only borderline value — AD, last digit, p = 0.02 (a deficit of digit 4) — has no “invented numbers” profile and is not significant after multiple-testing correction.

✓
Outliers vs neighbours

81 of 3,092 parishes (2.6%) beyond |z| > 4 — small parishes, mixed directions, no clustering at high turnout.

✓
Jumps versus the 2024 election

Not a single parish shows a simultaneous jump in turnout and in AD share above 15 points.

✓
Machine learning (Isolation Forest)

31 anomalies (top 1% by construction): tiny Azorean and mountain parishes with high blank-ballot shares; no suspicious clustering.

✓
Precinct protocols cross-checked against official totals

In six municipalities that publish precinct maps (91 precincts, 60,711 registered voters = 0.66% of the electorate), the precinct sums match the official totals of every list to the exact vote; the row arithmetic is exact; no duplicated protocols.

✓
Self-test: the toolkit actually detects fraud

Synthetic stuffing injections fire every alarm (Shpilkin signal ×18; round-number z 0.2 → 5.1). The “clean” verdict is a real measurement.

What this analysis cannot see

Honest limits of the method — statistics shows anomalies, not intentions.

  • The national analysis level is the parish: the Interior Ministry portal does not publish precinct-level results. The precinct check covers six municipalities (0.66% of the electorate); at that scale, the self-test shows the round-number test cannot detect redrawing of ~9% of protocols.
  • The overseas electorate (~1.6 million registered voters) is excluded — it is not part of the parish-level official export.
  • The Shpilkin method estimates magnitude under model assumptions; the anomaly boundary comes from the 2022/2024 control elections, not only from the shape of the distribution.
  • Digit tests are weak; the single borderline deviation (AD, p = 0.02) has no fraud profile and does not survive multiple-testing correction.
  • Statistics can confirm the numbers are mutually consistent; it cannot substitute documentary evidence (minutes, witnesses) or reveal mechanisms.
  • I am an independent researcher with no ties to any electoral body; all input files are official publications of the Portuguese authorities, and the analysis code is open source.

Data & reproducibility

  • Source: official results portal of the Ministry of Internal Administration at eleicoes.mai.gov.pt — parish-level export, 3,092 territories (mainland, Madeira, Azores).
  • Cross-checks: parish sums close exactly against the national totals; the precinct sums of the six publishing municipalities match the official totals to the exact vote.
  • Toolkit: open-source election-forensics pipeline (Python, pandas / scikit-learn) implementing Shpilkin, Klimek, Kobak, Beber–Scacco and other methods.
