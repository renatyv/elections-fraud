The result in context

Two snapshots, two purposes: the latest count and the reconciled forensic dataset.

Serbia elected a 250-seat National Assembly on 25 October 2026. The country is one constituency, with D’Hondt allocation, a 3% threshold and special rules for minority lists. The figures here are interim published results, not certified final mandates.

Three findings to keep in view

A counting-order reversal, a robust majority and a limited statistical verdict.

1

The early count was a different sample

Vučić led by 6.13 points at 9.38% counted. Students first lead between 25.45% and 41.35% counted. Belgrade was only about 2.9% counted initially; changing geographic coverage plausibly explains the reversal.

2

The seat majority survives the tested rules

At 16:55, Students receive 139–141 seats across the tested allocation rules, including 140 under the legal minority bonus. This sensitivity test holds the captured votes fixed.

3

Consistency is only part of an audit

All 18 list totals reconcile across levels. Yet reported voters exceed valid + invalid ballots by 2,650 at 16:55, across 565 stations. I retain this discrepancy for protocol follow-up; I do not assign it a cause.

•
How the lead changed through the count

14 snapshots: an early reversal, a 13.62-point peak, then a small narrowing.

At 22:04 CET on election night, Vučić led 48.01% to 41.88%. The first observed Students lead is bounded by the 23:07 and 00:08 captures. Their margin rose to 13.62 points at 16:55 and narrowed to 13.54 at 21:15. The full series is not monotonic. Hourly snapshots cannot exclude movements or corrections between captures.

Abroad and prisons need their own denominators

Abroad remains at 8/93 stations: Students have 89.57% of the displayed ballot total in that counted subset. Turnout is 80.06% of its covered register, but 11.91% of the full abroad register. Prisons are at 28/29 stations: covered-register turnout 89.24%, Vučić 65.61% of ballots. These special registers are excluded from territorial forensics. The remaining diaspora cannot be reliably extrapolated from eight stations.

•
What the electoral rules change

A 35% minority-quotient bonus matters for the projection. The winner stays the same.

The legal threshold is 3% of voters who voted. Minority lists are exempt, and those below 3% receive a 35% increase in their D’Hondt quotients. I include all nine minority lists. At the 16:55 snapshot, 171,010 valid votes (4.04%) went to non-minority lists below the threshold; SPS accounts for 92,761 of them.

A plurality of municipalities is not a majority of voters

Students lead in 90 territorial municipalities, Vučić in 94, other lists in five. Students nevertheless have 53.37% of territorial valid votes. Neither list wins a majority of municipalities. A separate counterfactual that apportions seats by region produces exactly the same 140/104/six totals at this snapshot.

The broad opposition grouping rises from 39.49% of valid votes in 2023 to 54.63% in 2026; Students alone have 53.44%. This is a descriptive comparison across changing parties, turnout and electorates. Aggregate results do not identify individual transfers or prove that consolidation caused the win.

Statistical checks · the reconciled 16:55 snapshot

Five forensic runs cover both grains and the historical control, plus a separate incumbent check. Open each test for its result and limits. Detailed diagnostic figures retain their English labels in both page languages.

•
High-turnout tails · Shpilkin

0.12% of Students’ votes beyond the 2023 station boundary; 0.31% using the current sample’s own boundary.

The 2023-controlled station boundary is 87.97% turnout. Its tail contains 2,762 Students votes; excluding all ballots there changes their voters-based share from 52.65% to 52.77%. The incumbent tail contains 8,670 votes (0.52%), moving his share 39.16% → 39.09%. Municipality aggregation gives a larger 4.29% Students tail but only a 0.07-point share shift. These are tail sensitivities, not counts of proven fraudulent ballots.

•
Turnout versus vote share · scatter and correlations

Students: +0.497 nationally; Vučić: −0.090. Within-region relationships differ.

Register-weighted turnout/share correlations are +0.497 for Students and −0.090 for Vučić. The incumbent is positively correlated with turnout in several regions despite the negative national correlation: geography changes the aggregate relationship. Socioeconomic, ethnic and station-size differences can confound the test. A national correlation cannot rule out local manipulation.

!
Two-component fingerprints

The Students mixture separates into two clusters; the incumbent mixture does not.

The Students station clusters centre around (59.0% turnout, 34.7% share) and (72.1%, 57.3%); the higher-turnout component has weight 0.746. This is a real heterogeneity flag. A component weight is not a fraud fraction. Geography and mobilization are plausible contributors, but the mixture model does not identify the cause. The 2023 incumbent mixture is not formally separated.

•
Round-number percentages · integer peaks

Turnout z = +0.90; Students’ share z = −3.48, a deficit of integers rather than heaping.

No excess of integer turnout is detected: z = +0.90, or −0.30 for multiples of five. The Students share statistic is −3.48 and the incumbent’s is −1.46. The negative Students value is a deviation, but not the round-number excess expected under this particular fabrication hypothesis. The 2023 integer-turnout statistic is −0.88.

!
Second-digit tests · Benford

SPS strongly rejects; Students and Vučić have smaller deviations. The assumed count distribution matters.

Theoretical station-level p-values are 0.0498 for Students, 0.0146 for Vučić and about 1.2×10⁻²⁶ for SPS. Only SPS survives a 5% Bonferroni check across six list/digit tests. Of its 3,844 eligible counts, 3,811 (99.14%) lie in 10–99, a poor setting for a universal Benford null. The fitted smooth null also rejects (Monte Carlo p = 0.00249), so this remains a model/data finding to investigate. It is not proof of fabricated tallies.

•
Last-digit tests

Students p = 0.2175; Vučić p = 0.1317; the SPS test has only 33 eligible counts.

Last-digit uniformity is tested only on station counts of at least 100. Students and Vučić do not reject at 5%. SPS has p = 0.4832 but a small eligible sample, limiting power. For the 2023 NOS list there are no eligible counts: the test is unavailable, not a passing result. Multiple testing and the count-generating process remain relevant.

•
Local outliers and Isolation Forest

146 of 8,124 stations flagged against their municipality; 136 of 8,163 in the historical control.

The local screen flags 1.80% of 2026 stations and 1.67% of 2023 stations at |robust z| > 4. Large z-scores can arise in small or locally homogeneous groups. Isolation Forest flags 82 stations in each year because its expected anomaly fraction is configured near 1%; that count is not independent evidence of cleanliness. Flagged stations are leads for document review.

•
The completed 2023 control and matched stations

All 8,273 historical stations captured; 7,935 territorial station keys match the 2026 sample.

The full 2023 capture has 8,163 territorial stations and 110 prison/abroad stations; all 18 list totals reconcile to the national result. Across matched territorial keys, turnout rises 58.63% → 68.83%, while the incumbent’s valid-vote share falls 47.92% → 39.83%. A matching station number does not prove unchanged boundaries or identify the same individuals. The 2023 election is a historical reference, not a certified clean benchmark.

•
Synthetic sensitivity check

Injected stuffing increases the own-boundary tail signal about 31 times.

On this dataset the synthetic injection increases the Students tail from 6,974 to 217,414 votes. Forced turnout rounding moves z from +0.90 to +3.22. This verifies sensitivity to the tested simulations. It does not measure power against all fraud types or prove the absence of manipulation in the original data.

•
What this analysis cannot establish

Interim published totals are evidence about the count, not a complete election audit.

  • The station forensics cover 97.24% of stations at 16:55 CET. Trends extend to 97.60% at 21:15. The outstanding count and later corrections can change final totals. The two snapshots are not silently mixed.
  • At 16:55, reported voters exceed valid + invalid ballots by 2,650; at 21:15, the gap is 2,656. These values are retained. Neither reconciliation of list totals nor a passing statistical test resolves this discrepancy.
  • No ballot papers, physical station protocols, voter-register quality, coercion, vote buying, campaign conditions or chain of custody were audited. Statistical consistency cannot certify that an election was free and fair.
  • Municipality registers include unprocessed stations; processed-station registers do not. Forensic party shares use reported voters; portal shares use valid + invalid ballots. Historical station keys do not establish identical electorates.
