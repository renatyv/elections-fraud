Independent statistical audit

India voted over seven phases in 2024. Do the numbers add up?

✓

Verdict: no statistical traces of manipulation — at the levels official data allows

I ran the standard election-forensics tests on every level India publishes: the official constituency results of all 542 contested constituencies, and — recovered from scanned Form 20 PDFs via the Wayback Machine — the booth-level count of 109,952 polling stations of Uttar Pradesh. Every applicable test is consistent with an honest count. The honest caveat: India publishes no per-station electorate for 2024, so the turnout-based booth tests are impossible this year — see “What this analysis cannot see”.

What was decided in April–June 2024

General election to the Lok Sabha (543 seats, seven phases from 19 April to 1 June). The BJP won 240 seats with 36.5% of the vote; the Indian National Congress 99 seats with 21.2%. The NDA returned to government with a reduced majority. National turnout: 66.1% — 646 million ballots.

Surat was won without a poll (unopposed) and has no count. The booth-level recovery covers Uttar Pradesh — 80 constituencies, 12.6% of the national electorate — which is also the state every serious 2024 fraud debate concentrated on.

Three reasons the numbers can be trusted — at these levels

1

The high-turnout zone is empty

Constituency turnout is one bell curve that dies out by ~80%. Beyond the 87.6% boundary — the upper percentile of the honest 2014 election, not fitted to 2024 — stand zero votes: not a single constituency, nationally or among Uttar Pradesh's assembly segments. Ballot-stuffing shifts mass precisely into that 88–100% zone.

2

The winner's result does not grow with turnout

In the turnout × result scatter the BJP cloud slopes slightly DOWN (weighted correlation −0.12 nationally, −0.29 in UP's assembly segments) — the comet-tail signature of stuffing is a strongly positive slope. The positive correlations that do exist belong to regional opposition parties in their own strongholds: ordinary voter heterogeneity.

3

110,000 recovered UP stations behave like the honest control

The counting records (Form 20) of 109,952 polling stations — scanned PDFs recovered from the Wayback Machine, transcribed and arithmetically checksummed row by row — show flat last-digit distributions and no excess of round-number shares. The same tests on the honest 2014 booths give the same picture; and the self-test proves the toolkit detects injected fraud (signal ×17.6 million).

How I checked

The same families of tests used on this site for Slovenia, Brazil and Portugal, run with open-source code on official ECI exports plus the recovered Form 20 tables. Summary: all clean at the available levels.

✓
Anomalous-vote estimate (Shpilkin method)

Beyond the honest-decline boundary: zero votes for the leader — nationally and in UP. Recounting without the anomaly zone moves nothing.

The Shpilkin method models the honest bell curve of turnout and measures the votes beyond its decline. The boundary (87.6%) is not fitted to 2024: it is the 99.5th percentile of the 2014 election's elector-weighted turnout. To its right stand zero constituencies and zero votes; the “result without the anomalous zone” (36.73%) differs from the observed one (36.50%) only because there is nothing to remove. In Uttar Pradesh's 81 assembly segments with published electors: boundary 82.8% (from UP-2014), tail zero.

✓
Turnout × result scatter and fingerprints

No “comet tail”, no separated second cluster in the 2D fingerprint — the structure that appears is geography and alliance arithmetic.

In a manipulated count the turnout × result cloud stretches up-right: high-turnout units carry a sharply higher leader share. Here BJP's correlation with turnout is negative (−0.12 nationally, −0.29 in UP segments). The Klimek fingerprint (2D density of turnout × leader share) finds no separated cluster; the second Gaussian component (weight 0.18) is degenerate — pinned near zero share. The correlations that are positive (AITC +0.38, YSRCP +0.33) belong to regional parties whose strongholds vote more — mirrored between government and opposition, i.e. heterogeneity, not direction.

✓
Round-number peaks (Kobak et al.)

No excess of exactly-integer turnouts or leader shares — at the constituency level, and across 105,000 recovered UP stations the integer-share rate is BELOW expectation (z = −3.1).

Numbers invented at a desk cluster on round values. Among 542 constituencies, 1.11% have exactly integer turnout vs 0.98% expected (z = +0.3) — at ~1.8M electors per unit the test is nearly vacuous, and reported only for completeness. Where the data is fine-grained it is clean too: across the recovered UP stations, 7.85% have an exactly integer BJP share vs 7.95% expected from the binomial null — a deficit, the opposite of the desk-drawn signature. The honest 2014 booth control gives the same picture (turnout peaks 2.02% vs 2.05%, z = −1.1).

✓
Digit tests (Benford, last digit)

Last digits flat everywhere. The only borderline value — SP's last digit in UP booths (p = 0.03, +0.4 pp on 0s) — is an order of magnitude smaller than invented-number signatures and mirrors 2014's benign profile.

Benford's law on second digits rejects its smooth-count null at booth level (p ≈ 0.002, deviation ≤1.3 pp) — but identically for all four major parties and identically in the honest 2014 control: a stable structural property of UP's booth-size mixture (urban/rural regimes), not a party-specific fabrication trace. Last-digit uniformity — the classic Beber–Scacco test — holds for BJP (p = 0.46), INC (0.25) and BSP (0.06); SP shows the single mildest deviation of this whole analysis (+0.37 pp on trailing 0s, −0.25 pp on 8s; p_mc = 0.027), an order of magnitude below documented fabrication effects. Worth re-checking when the remaining ACs are recovered — not a fraud finding.

✓
Outliers, machine learning, and the swing vs 2014

19 of 542 constituencies (3.5%) are neighbour-outliers and 6 are ML-anomalies — small and scattered, audit candidates by construction. Not one constituency combines a 15-point turnout jump with a 15-point leader jump against 2014.

Each constituency is a z-score against the median of its own state: 19 exceed |z| > 4 — an ordinary tail, mixed directions, no clustering. 507 constituencies matched by name between 2014 and 2024: zero double-jumps (turnout AND leader share up sharply together), the sharpest cross-election manipulation signature. The Δ-turnout/Δ-share cloud is structureless.

✓
The Form 20 recovery itself: arithmetically exact

1.16 million station-candidate rows parsed under per-row checksums; against the official ECI constituency totals nothing exceeds the official count anywhere, and 11 fully-recovered constituencies match within ±0.5%.

The UP Form 20s are scans and prints scattered across 54 district websites, most of them gone; everything was recovered from the Wayback Machine and a government CDN. Every parsed row must satisfy sum(candidates) = valid and valid + NOTA + rejected = total under exactly one reading of the table layout; rows that fail are withheld, not guessed. Aggregated and compared with the official ECI export: every delta is ≤ 0 — the recovery never counts more votes than the official result — and where the source files are complete the match is within half a percent. Party labels for candidate columns were assigned by matching each constituency's column sums to the official candidate totals (validated <5% deviation wherever station coverage exceeds 90%).

✓
Self-test: the toolkit actually detects fraud

Synthetic stuffing injected into the clean data fires every alarm (anomaly-zone signal from 0 to 17.6M votes; round-number z from 0.4 to 11.8). The “clean” verdict is a measurement, not a blind spot.

A clean verdict is only meaningful if the tools can fire. Injecting additive stuffing into the 2024 constituencies grows the anomalous-zone estimate from zero to 17.6 million votes and drives the integer-peak statistic from z = 0.4 to 11.8; in the UP assembly segments the signal reaches 0.87M; at 2014 booth level the tail multiplies ×7. On this data the machinery would see millions of stuffed ballots — it sees none.

What this analysis cannot see

Honest limits — statistics shows anomalies, not intentions; and here it also has honest blind spots.

  • Granularity. The national tests run on 542 units of ~1.8 million electors each. Fraud concentrated in a few hundred booths inside one constituency is statistically invisible at that level.
  • No booth-level turnout for 2024. The counting record (Form 20) carries no per-station electorate — I verified this in the raw documents — so the flagship turnout tests at station level are impossible for 2024. The 2014 election, where the ECI did publish per-booth electors, serves as the booth-level baseline instead. Finding Form 17C / per-station electorate data would convert every turnout method to 110,000-station resolution.
  • The UP segment subset is not random. Electorate numbers survive in the forms of only 81 of 297 recovered segments (a header field, often left blank); selection correlates with district administration type.
  • Recovery coverage. 297 of 403 assembly segments recovered; where source PDFs are partial the sums fall short of the official totals (never exceed them). Party labels in four low-coverage constituencies are approximate.
  • Digit tests are weak instruments; their borderline values here are smaller than fabrication signatures by an order of magnitude and replicate in the honest 2014 control.
  • Statistics can confirm the numbers are mutually consistent; it cannot substitute documentary evidence (agents' copies of Form 17C, VVPAT audits) or reveal mechanisms. I am an independent researcher with no ties to any electoral body; all inputs are official ECI publications or public archives, and the code is open source.

Data & reproducibility

  • National results: official ECI constituencywise detailed export (in-rolls), 542 constituencies, cross-checked against the results portal and a Kaggle mirror.
  • UP booth data: 315 Form 20 PDFs (Encore digital + scans) recovered from the Wayback Machine and cdn.s3waas.gov.in, transcribed (OCR where needed) and checksum-parsed — dataset and build scripts in the repository.
  • Controls: ECI 2014 constituency and booth exports (the one cycle with per-booth electors); 2019 has no electorate field and is not used.
  • Toolkit: the same open-source election-forensics pipeline used on this site for Slovenia, Brazil and Portugal (Shpilkin, Klimek, Kobak, Beber–Scacco, neighbours, ML).
