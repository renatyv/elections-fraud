What happened on 25 October 2026

Brazil held the runoff of its presidential election: Lula (PT) against Flávio Bolsonaro (PL), the two leaders of the 4 October first round. Voting is compulsory and electronic; the electoral authority TSE publishes final results per section — 499,248 of them. Counting finished about four hours after polls closed; the losing candidate publicly disputed the result, which makes an independent check of the numbers all the more useful.

The runoff as a natural experiment

Three weeks separate two votes on the same 499,248 sections — first round with 13 candidates, runoff with two. Aggregate section totals can estimate where votes moved, but these are ecological estimates, not tracked voters; the same data cannot prove how any individual voted.

1

The scenario missed by 4.36 pp

Applying 2022 transfer rates to the 2026 first round gave Flávio Bolsonaro 52.6% of the two-candidate vote. The actual runoff gave Lula 51.79%. The bootstrap interval (±0.03 pp) covered model resampling only — the real uncertainty sat in the mapping and in voters changing behaviour.

2

The eliminated candidates’ voters split differently than in 2022

In 2022, voters of the third-placed Tebet were estimated to go 49.9% to Bolsonaro and 27.9% to Lula. In 2026, the voters of Cury, Ferreira and Caiado — the three eliminated heavyweights — actually split ⟦TR_SUMMARY⟧ of their two-candidate vote to Lula. The 2022 pattern did not repeat.

3

A smooth count, an uneven reporting order

Bolsonaro led from roughly 2% to 90% counted; Lula finished ahead. The 11 states that crossed 50% counted first average 46.7% for Lula two-way; the other 17 average 54.0%. The trajectory follows the reporting order — no discontinuity appears in it.

Check · The scenario against the actual result

The round-1 analysis mapped 2026 candidates to 2022 vote categories by geographic similarity and carried the 2022 transfer rates over unchanged. The runoff is now a direct test of that scenario: every input came from the first round, the outcome from 25 October.

The bootstrap interval (47.39–47.45% for Lula) quantified resampling of the fitted 2022 model, nothing more. It excluded candidate-mapping uncertainty and any change in how voters behave between elections. The 4.36 pp miss measures those excluded parts.

Counting · One evening, two leads

Thirteen snapshots of the TSE results portal from 17:07 BRT (polls close) to the final tally at 23:26 BRT. Electronic counting is fast; the visible swings track which states transmit first — small northern states early, the Southeast’s giant pools later.

The shape — converge, cross, diverge — is the reporting order at work, not a changing count. The first 0.2% was mostly fast-counted sections abroad, which lean Lula; the near-tie around 20:00 reflects the fast-transmitting, Bolsonaro-leaning South/Southeast states dominating the sample; the final 3.6-point gap reappears once the late, Lula-leaning Northeast pools arrive. A mid-evening near-tie simply means the accumulated mix happened to match the national mix — the end of the line is the actual margin.

A “blue shift” or “red shift” during counting is a reporting-order phenomenon, common to many jurisdictions (absentee and large urban pools report late). It is evidence about sequence, not about the integrity of the count; the final totals cross-check against the per-section dataset to the exact vote.

Scope and limitations

This is an analysis of published aggregate results, not a procedural audit of voting machines or ballot handling. The 2022 comparison is a reference election, not a known fraud-free ground truth. Statistical non-detection does not prove absence of manipulation; test assumptions, spatial dependence and multiple comparisons matter. Aggregate transfer estimates do not identify individual behaviour — a section-level split does not establish who any voter supported, and good aggregate fit does not validate individual rates.

Data & reproducibility

  • Source: official section-level (seção) results from the TSE open-data portal (resultados-2026), files regenerated after the runoff, filter NR_TURNO=2: ⟦D1⟧ sections including abroad voting.
  • Self-consistency: ⟦D2⟧ The national totals match the election-night portal capture and press figures to the exact vote. Control dataset: TSE resultados-2022, both rounds, matched to the same sections.
  • Toolkit: open-source election-forensics pipeline (Python, pandas / scikit-learn) implementing Shpilkin, Klimek, Kobak, Beber–Scacco, neighbour-outlier and Isolation-Forest methods, urna-model stratification, the 2022 controls and synthetic self-tests.
  • Full technical report (in Russian), the section dataset, scripts and all figures and tables are available in the project repository; election-night trend capture (13 snapshots) included.
  • First-round analysis, geographic grouping, modelled 2022 transfers and the conditional scenario: see the round-1 page and PARADOXES.md. Interpret all outputs with the limitations stated above.
